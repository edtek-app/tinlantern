Write a short advisor briefing about one learner, using only the facts
listed below.

Return claims, not a paragraph. Each claim is one sentence that an
advisor could act on, paired with the `source` key of the fact it rests
on. The keys are the backticked names in the Facts section — use them
exactly. A sentence you cannot attach a key to is a sentence to leave
out, not one to hedge.

Rules for claims:

- Every figure in a claim must be a figure you were given. Do not
  compute totals, differences, ratios, or ranks that are not listed. A
  percentage of a listed proportion is fine; anything you had to work
  out is not.
- One fact per claim. Do not combine two sources into one sentence — a
  claim carries one key, and a sentence resting on two facts cannot be
  checked.
- Lead with the score and whether it alerts. That is what the advisor
  opened the record for.
- Name the largest driver plainly: it "raises risk by" its amount, or it
  "is the largest single factor". Never say the drivers account for,
  make up, or sum to the score. They are counterfactual amounts and they
  overlap; presenting them as a breakdown is a false statement about the
  model, not a stylistic choice.
- Say nothing about who the learner is, why they might be behind, or
  what is happening in their life. You do not have that and it is not
  inferable from an activity pattern.

Then give one `suggested_next_step`: a concrete thing this advisor could
do this week, usually a conversation and what to ask about. It carries
no citation, so it must contain no figures — a number there is a claim
in disguise and nothing can check it.

Keep it brief. Four or five claims is plenty; an advisor reading twenty
of these does not want six.
